Proof is hard to find.

  1. 01

    A heading with no results under it.

    "Real Customers, Real Results." holds three quotes about partnership and support. One is from a marketing agency, not an operator.

  2. 02

    One hard number in 15 case studies.

    Fossil Rim: 65% more business with 55% less staffing, since May 2020. It never reaches the homepage.

  3. 03

    Reviews and security are quiet.

    Capterra shows 4.4 from 22 reviews, none newer than mid 2024. There is no security page. ROLLER leads with "3,500+" in its headline.

Buyers can't find it.

RocketRez wins searches for its own name and loses the ones buyers type when they don't know it yet.

  1. 0

    Of six AI answers named RocketRez.

    Best zoo ticketing software, ROLLER alternatives, what is per-cap spending, and three more. The answers leaned on review sites and on comparison pages competitors wrote.

  2. 5

    Footer industry links return Page Not Found.

    Museums, Scenic Railways, Wildlife Parks, Race Car Experiences, Towers and Observatories. "Museum ticketing software" draws 170 searches a month at a $19 cost per click.

  3. 6

    Of 11 links in llms.txt are dead.

    The file written for AI engines sends them to missing pages and a boat-boarding figure that conflicts with RocketRez's own case study.

Every path ends in one long form.

Nine required fields, including phone and annual revenue, and an option labelled "Resort (No ICP included)", an internal note buyers can see. Seven names for one button, from "Schedule a Demo" to "Consult Solutions Team". And no way to see the product without talking to sales.

The best line is below the fold.

  1. 01

    "Per-cap like a scoreboard" is a subtitle.

    RocketRez's most ownable idea is the one fewest visitors see.

  2. 02

    The market is described five ways.

    Mid-market tours, enterprise attractions, mid-to-large operators, $1M to $200M, $2M to $100M. A $4M zoo can't tell if it's the core customer.

  3. 03

    Words every rival uses.

    The live copy's own words, "Seamless", "world class" and "Revolutionize", appear on 204 lines. Typos sit in headline claims: "PowerBl", "Al-powered".

  4. 04

    The product reads as decoration.

    The hero's product cards can't be read at 1440 px or on a phone.

The housekeeping is quick.

  • The sitemap lists 367 URLs, including a test homepage, a 9.6 MB demo store and five account pages.
  • Titles follow three patterns; descriptions run to 221 characters and get cut off in results.
  • The homepage's main image paints at 4.4 s on throttled mobile, against 2.5 s for "good". Most of the 3 MB is tracking and form scripts.
  • Feature labels sit at 1.54:1 contrast; WCAG 2.2 asks for 4.5:1.
  • Eleven menu headings come before the page's own heading for screen reader users.
  • On a phone, the demo form starts 1.6 screens down the page whose only job is the form.

Every measurement, finding and source is in audit.md.

Sell the whole day, not just the ticket.

The concept site is built on one sentence: tickets, food, retail, memberships and guest texting on one system, so every guest's spend adds up to one number an operator can grow. That number is per-cap.

Five systems, one guest.

  1. 01

    Per-cap is the number the whole site points at.

    Buyers already keep score this way, and one number is easier to remember than eighteen modules. One asset used consistently is what makes a brand easy to recall (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute).

  2. 02

    The hero is one guest's day.

    A ticket, lunch, a photo and a membership upgrade land on a timeline and the counter adds them up. About 57% of viewing time sits above the fold (NN/g).

  3. 03

    Five systems collapse into one record.

    It shows buyers their own Monday morning and argues against doing nothing. Operators say it themselves: two systems were "creating chaos between our guests and our staff" (Hemker Park and Zoo).

Real screens, one point each.

Today the same point-of-sale screen appears on the homepage and the Ticketing page. The concept rebuilds each screen in HTML with sample data, so it stays sharp and can be clicked.

Decisions 4 and 5. Let hesitant buyers see the product without a sales call, and let them learn by doing (NN/g, B2B usability; complex application design). One callout per screen and a benefit headline on every card: the main point first, the detail after (NN/g, inverted pyramid).

Grouped by the operator's job.

Eighteen module pages fold into four jobs an operator recognises, Sell, Serve, Keep and Know, with Relay guest texting as its own item.

Decision 6. Operators think "sell more before they arrive" and "bring them back", not "Upsells and Cross Sells". Use the user's words, not internal category names (NN/g, usability heuristic 2), and give every group a name the reader understands.

Every venue gets a page.

Each venue page opens with what that operator fights, in their terms. A zoo's problem, members at every till, is not a boat tour's, so tours get their own headline: "Fill every departure, from every channel."

Decision 7. Segment by how customers describe themselves (NN/g, B2B navigation). Buyers recall brands through their own buying situation, "software for our zoo", so each page ties RocketRez to one situation (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute).

Proof with names and dates.

Real logos, real venue photos, quotes with a name and title, and results with a date. Fossil Rim's result always carries "since its May 2020 launch".

Decision 8. Testimonials earn trust with the person's title and company (NN/g). Unknown brands need reassurance, and named specifics beat generic claims (Baymard Institute). An undated result invites a CFO to discount it.

Every fee, shown up front.

Pricing becomes a quote builder: two choices, about a minute, and the layout of a real quote with every fee line in it, plus the contract terms to get in writing.

Decision 10. Price is the information B2B visitors rank highest, and hiding it reads as evasive (NN/g). An honest note: NN/g also finds a starting price or range beats a calculator. The page is built to take one the day RocketRez publishes it.

Answers for the whole committee.

Comparisons that admit rival strengths

Dated sources, "Not stated" where the record is silent. Balanced proof beats glowing proof (NN/g).

Security your IT team can check

What's published, and what's sent on request. Never a badge for something not held (Baymard).

Switching around the opening day

Enter an opening day, get a start date with the maths shown, plus an outline for the board (NN/g, B2B).

A Relay calculator with nothing hidden

Every take rate and price is editable, labelled a starting guess. A CFO trusts a model they can argue with.

Decisions 9, 11, 12 and 13. RocketRez appeared in none of six AI answers to buyer questions, partly because only competitors had written comparison pages.

Learn, built for questions.

The Academy, the Signal podcast, guides, blog and webinars move into one searchable library of about 270 items, with nothing basic behind a form.

Decisions 14 to 16. Help should be searchable and task-focused, and how-to content shouldn't sit behind forms (NN/g). Plain operator voice, with category words such as "seamless" and "all-in-one" banned: concise, objective copy measured 124% more usable than promotional copy (NN/g).

One button, down to a phone.

  1. 20

    "Book a demo", everywhere.

    Five fields, and the form says who replies and what happens next. NN/g and Baymard both point to 3 to 5 fields.

  2. 19

    WCAG 2.2 AA, down to 390 px.

    4.5:1 text contrast, 24 px targets, keyboard paths, reduced motion. In the WebAIM Million 2026, 95.9% of home pages had a detectable failure.

  3. 17

    Seven type sizes, ten spacing steps.

    An audit script fails the build on anything off-scale. About three type sizes per view keeps emphasis meaningful (NN/g).

What we left out on purpose.

01

AI imagery

Every photo is a real customer venue from Wikimedia Commons, credited. When anyone can generate a perfect image, a real one earns trust.

02

Raster screenshots

Every product view is a working recreation of a real screen, labelled sample data.

03

Invented numbers

No customer count, uptime, per-cap lift or price RocketRez hasn't published. Where one belongs, the page is built to take it.

04

Fake urgency

No countdowns or "only 3 spots left". The only deadline is the operator's own opening day.

Claims and search. Every number traces to rocketrez.com or a named customer. The Relay growth quote is attributed to H Two Marketing, the park's agency, not the park. Titles use buyer search terms, with one heading per page and answer-first sections. All 22 decisions and their sources are in why.md.

What the concept is waiting on

  • Customer count and countries, and tickets or dollars processed a year
  • A per-cap lift across three or more customers, with the method
  • Confirmation that Insights reports spend per guest by department
  • Support response median, uptime history and a status page
  • Security attestations: SOC 2 status, PCI level, SSO and MFA, hosting region
  • A starting price or range, and contract terms
  • Confirmation of the 8 to 12 week go-live and the demo reply window
  • More logo files, staff portraits, and permission for venue photos and quotes
  • The guide PDFs behind the Learn forms, and a fresh round of reviews
