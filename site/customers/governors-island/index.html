Home / Customers / Governors Island

Four kiosks let Governors Island sell ferry tickets without a night agent.

A public park with up to 20,000 weekend visitors moved ferry ticketing from handhelds at the boat to online, phone, kiosk and agent sales.

Attraction
Public park ferry
Where
Eight hundred yards from lower Manhattan, New York
Replaced
Handheld ticketing devices with card readers, with tickets sold only as guests boarded the boat

In this story

BeforeThe moveAfter

Inside the story

  • Four self-serve kiosks at the ticket booth for low-traffic hours
  • Cash accepted at every kiosk through a custom development project
  • One back-end dashboard shows every sales channel

Before

Governors Island sees roughly 2,000 visitors per weekday and up to 20,000 each weekend. The manual process was limited by how many tickets agents could sell while also answering phones. COVID-19 social distancing raised the worry that sales capacity and an inclusive buying experience would both suffer.

The move

Online, phone and point-of-sale purchases now run from one system with real-time capacity rules. Four kiosks went in at the ticket booth. Cash payment was built into every kiosk because the island is a public park.

“We are a public park and want equitable access, so we need to ensure there are options so that people who have no credit card can purchase in cash.” Robert Lomangino, Operations Project Manager

After

Ticket agents no longer stay late into the night and spend their time answering questions instead. Data is tracked across the whole platform, so reports on sales and profitability are easier to run.

“[We love] having an interactive back-end dashboard that allows us to adapt our ticketing offers to our changing operations. We’re seasonal, weekend-focused, often have special events, and activities can extend late into the night. Having a system that can all work together and change as we need it to is great for the future.” Robert Lomangino, Operations Project Manager
